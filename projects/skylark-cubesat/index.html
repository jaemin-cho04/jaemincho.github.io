CubeSat Remote-Station Verification

Engineering analysis for the solar-powered Motus receiver stations that support Western Skylark CubeSat operations in London, Moosonee, and Churchill.

Engineering resultTemperature capability and normal winter charging passed; the analysis identified a battery-autonomy shortfall under a seven-day, zero-solar blackout.

Organization
Western Skylark CubeSat
Period
2025 — 2026
System
Remote Motus receiver
Methods
Datasheet review, duty-cycle modeling, worst-case analysis
Goal

Verify temperature, charging, and seven-day stored-power requirements before deployment.

What I produced

Two traceable engineering records covering component operating limits and a full-year power-supply analysis.

Result

Two requirements passed; one conditional pass exposed a 4.31-day blackout limit and drove concrete mitigation options.

Decision summary

Three requirements, one clear risk

The analysis distinguishes normal winter operation from a complete solar blackout. The panel can replenish the daily load in the modeled winter case, but one cold-derated battery cannot sustain seven days without solar input.

RS-42 · TemperaturePass-30 to 85 °C

Verified electronics range versus a -29 to 26 °C expected station envelope.

RS-24 · ChargingPass360 Wh/day

Modeled worst-location winter generation versus 252.33 Wh/day demand.

RS-25 · AutonomyConditional4.31 of 7 days

Cold-derated battery endurance under a complete zero-solar blackout.

How it worked

From component limits to system decisions

Each review began with a written requirement, documented the source assumptions, performed the calculation, and ended with an explicit result and recommendation.

Evidence

Key inputs and calculated outputs

Recommendations from the gap

  • Add a second or larger battery.
  • Reduce idle draw or introduce a sub-5 W low-power mode.
  • Use subsystem shutdown logic during extended low-solar periods.
  • Consider snow-mitigation hardware and complete environmental testing before deployment.
Scope and ownership

I authored the RS-24 and RS-25 power-supply verification and revised it from team feedback. I also contributed the RS-42 temperature verification with two teammates. This case study covers the remote ground station—not the spacecraft—and reports analytical verification; environmental and field testing remain follow-up work.
